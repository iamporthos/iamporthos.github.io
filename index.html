Springy

Mila — constraint to joinery

One hard rule: no screws, no glue, no fasteners of any kind. Every connection solved in the geometry of the wood, then CNC-milled and hand-assembled. Sketches → renders → milled parts.

The full journey

A painter in 2015 who now load-tests furniture. Every stop was sketched, then built for real — open any piece to read the full drawing set.

fig. one of ten
2015
✦ Art

Futuristic Koiworld

A painting of a 3D koi pond on a vertical board — flat surface, dimensional illusion. Before any product work, this was the first lesson in making people stop and look.

Made people walk closer to a flat board to check whether the water was real — the first proof that perceived depth is what earns a second look, a lesson every product since has leaned on.

fig. two of ten
2015
✦ Client Work

Eat Drink Make Art

A flyer designed for the Eat Drink Make Art event: template, final flyer, and an in-context mockup. The first time my work had a deadline, a client, and a job to do.

Approved by my manager and used for the event — and because I built a reusable template rather than a one-off, the next flyer didn't need a designer at all.

fig. three of ten
2018
✦ Branding · Self-directed

The Grind

A self-set branding brief for an imagined coffee shop: exploratory sketches, the final logo, and a mockup of the mark in context. An early sign that identity work would become a throughline.

A self-directed brief that forced the hardest identity constraint: one mark that stays readable shrunk onto a cup and blown up onto signage — so a shop owner would never need a second version.

fig. four of ten
2018
✦ Industrial Design

Dost

A phone and tablet stand made from cardboard with a rubber grip — documented from first sketch to final working prototype. The project where industrial design clicked.

Gives someone a stable viewing angle for a phone or tablet out of cardboard and a rubber grip — a working stand at effectively no material cost, and proof the geometry holds before anyone spends on tooling.

fig. five of ten
2019
✦ Industrial Design

LuvMy

A child's watering can taken through the complete design process: concept sketches, a technical drawing, a how-to-use diagram, and finished renders.

Made watering a plant achievable for a child: scaled grip and pour weight turned a chore an adult had to do into something a kid wants to do.

fig. six of ten
2023
✦ Concept

2 Ball

A game concept that puts two basketballs in play at once — mockups plus a worked-out rule set. Designing a system instead of an object.

Lets a group get an entirely new game out of a court they already have — the only thing anyone needs to buy is a second ball.

fig. seven of ten
2025
✦ Industrial Design

SHIFT

Sketches and renders for a water bottle concept, developed during formal industrial design training. The return to product design, sharper than before.

Makes a bottle someone actually wants to carry — the difference between a bottle that stays in a cupboard and one that leaves the house, achieved through form alone, with no added parts or cost.

fig. eight of ten
2025
✦ Industrial Design

Mila

A small-scale chair held together by nothing but its own joinery — no screws, no glue, no external fasteners. Sketches, renders, and video of the CNC-milled wooden pieces.

Zero screws, zero glue: fewer parts to source, nothing to loosen, and a flat-packable chair a customer can assemble and disassemble without tools.

fig. nine of ten
2025
✦ Industrial Design

Ballin

Version one of activewear eyewear frames, designed around pre-supplied lenses. A masterclass in working from a fixed constraint outward.

Designed around lenses I didn't control — the realistic case for most products — and produced a wearable prototype plus documented v2 fixes instead of a dead end.
